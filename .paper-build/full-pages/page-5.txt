The four ensembles do not obtain a clear advantage from 
their additional flexibility. Random Forest has the largest 
RMSE, while Extra Trees has a lower RMSE than XGBoost 
but a higher MAPE. These differences illustrate why a 
ranking based on one error metric is incomplete. Large states 
contribute strongly to squared error, whereas smaller states 
can dominate relative error. A model intended for state -
specific use should be assessed against the relevant error 
tolerance rather than chosen solely from the poole d 
leaderboard. 
B. What the Baselines Reveal 
The state-mean benchmark has RMSE of INR 17,552.79 
crore, only slightly above the linear model. The linear model's 
RMSE improvement over that benchmark is approximately 
0.81%, and its MAE is slightly worse. The persistence 
benchmark is substantially stronger: RMSE is INR 10,890.73 
crore, MAE is INR 5,343.19 crore, and R² is 0.9524. Its 
RMSE is approximately 37.45% lower than the linear 
model's. Table IV and Fig. 3 place the model results beside 
these benchmarks.

This comparison changes the interpretation of the 
apparent model fit. The climate -and-state regressors capture 
much of the difference between states, but they do not 
outperform the latest known state level. Historical averages 
can lag a changing economic series; a recent observation may 
therefore provide more useful information than the present 
weather features. This is consistent with the result, but the 
experiment does not isolate the contribution of technological 
change, cropping patterns, or other omitted economic drivers. 
The persistence result is also relevant to application 
design. “Best model” in the stored five -model comparison 
means the lowest RMSE within that particular candidate list. 
Once an additional benchmark is evaluated, the label must 
not be generalized into “ best available forecast.” A 
subsequent model -selection protocol should include 
persistence as a formal candidate and reserve a new 
evaluation period if the current results are used to guide 
feature or hyperparameter changes. 
C. Temporal and State-Level Error Patterns 
Figure 4 compares the linear model's predictions with 
observed GSVA. The wide range of state economic levels 
accounts for much of the visual spread. Large -state 
underprediction contributes substantial absolute error: over 
the held -out observations, Uttar P radesh has MAE of 
approximately INR 59,135.75 crore, Madhya Pradesh INR 
43,012.03 crore, and Maharashtra INR 27,432.86 crore. 
These are prediction errors, not measured climate losses. 
Differences in scale mean that a smaller state's lower absolute 
error need not indicate a better relative prediction.

Annual linear -model RMSE rises from INR 13,072.53 
crore in 2021–22 to INR 23,158.59 crore in 2024 –25. Table 
V and Fig. 5 show the progression. Increasing error with 
distance from the training period is consistent with a model 
that does not fully track evolving output levels. However, the 
state composition also changes: the final year contains 22 
observations rather than 28. The annual comparison therefore 
cannot attribute all deterioration to elapsed time alone.
