Small magnitudes limit substantive interpretation. 
The negative ONI value does not show that El Niño 
benefits agriculture. It shows that one particular perturbation 
procedure slightly improves one fitted model's prediction 
error on a short test window. Conversely, a small positive 
temperature value does no t quantify the economic damage 
caused by heat. The result concerns the sensitivity of the 
model's performance to its inputs. Aggregation, correlated 
predictors, shared annual variation, and a short record all 
constrain the interpretation. 
The diagnostic also discourages interpreting normalized 
absolute linear coefficients as percentage contributions to the 
economy. Those coefficients combine millimeters, degrees 
Celsius, and binary indicators, so their relative magnitudes 
depend on units. Tree importances describe the fitted splitting 
rules and face different limitations. Permutation is useful here 
because it assesses prediction error on held -out rows, but it 
still cannot replace a causal design or recover information 
removed by annual aggregation. 
VI. LIMITATIONS 
The first limitation is spatial representation. A single 
nearest grid point cannot characterize the full distribution of 
weather across a large state. Area -weighted climate 
extraction over administrative boundaries, preferably 
weighted by cultivated area o r crop distribution where 
justified, would provide a closer match to the state -level 
economic target. The current approach is computationally 
simple and auditable, but it should not be described as a state-
wide rainfall measurement. 
The second limitation is temporal resolution. Financial -
year rainfall and temperature averages can obscure monsoon 
breaks, planting dates, flowering -stage heat, and crop -
specific exposure. The same annual total can arise from 
different rainfall sequences. ONI is also shared across states, 
yielding only 14 distinct annual values before the split and 
only four in the test period. Hundreds of state -year rows 
therefore do not supply hundreds of independent ENSO 
histories. 
The third limitation concerns model inputs and validation. 
The feature set omits lagged GSVA, irrigation, crop area, 
crop composition, soil conditions, policy, and technology. 
The strong persistence benchmark makes lagged economic 
information a priority fo r future assessment. The current 
single chronological holdout does not measure stability 
across alternative cutoffs, and the reported model ranking has 
not been validated on a second untouched period. Repeatedly 
redesigning the system around the present te st results would 
weaken their status as an independent evaluation. 
Finally, the outcome is an aggregate economic measure at 
a constant price base. It cannot directly establish household 
welfare, crop -specific yield loss, or district drought 
vulnerability. Scenario outputs inherit the model's limitations 
and may involve im plausible combinations of inputs. 
Transparent models and clearly stated decision boundaries 
are particularly valuable when predictions could influence 
consequential choices [48]. At its present stage, the 
application supports exploratory analysis and repro ducible 
comparison, with predictive uncertainty and economic 
attribution remaining open research problems. 
VII. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 
The project integrates IMD weather observations, 
historical ONI, and RBI/NSO crop GSVA into a 385 -row 
state-year panel and a working analytical application. Under 
a chronological split, ordinary least squares achieves the 
lowest RMSE among five fitted regressors. However, a fixed 
last-observed-value benchmark performs substantially better, 
and climate permutation produces only small changes in 
linear-model error. The evidence supports a useful baseline 
for further research rather than a validated estimate of ENSO-
induced economic loss.
